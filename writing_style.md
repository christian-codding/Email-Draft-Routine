# Writing Style — Christian Codding

---

## Greetings

- **Strangers / professionals:** `Hi [Name],` or `Hi there,` — comma after, never an exclamation point on the opener
- **People you know:** `Hey [Name] -` with a dash, not a comma (e.g., "Hey Megan -", "Hey Zeus -")
- **Very casual / close contacts (e.g., Sofia):** No greeting at all — just dive in ("attached both", "Check plz")
- **Never use** "Dear," — ever

---

## Tone & Voice

- Warm but efficient — friendly without being formal or flowery
- Conversational and direct — say what you need and move on
- Never stiff or corporate-sounding
- Occasional personality in casual contexts ("That's badass, sign me up. Get one for me too?")
- Honest and matter-of-fact when correcting: "Honest mistake, there is no issue here. Thank you for getting back to me!"

---

## Sentence Structure

- Short sentences — often just one or two lines per email
- No filler or padding — never "I hope this email finds you well" or anything like that
- Questions are direct: "Are you able to send over what's available currently?"
- Stack info in prose, not lists — occasionally use exclamation points mid-email as a soft break

---

## Punctuation & Formatting

- Exclamation points used freely but not excessively — typically one per email, sometimes two
- No bullet points or numbered lists in replies — pure prose
- Commas over semicolons, always
- Dash ` - ` as a soft pause in casual emails ("Hey Megan - I will not be free but...")
- No ellipses in professional contexts; occasional use in casual ones

---

## Sign-offs

- **Professional / semi-casual:** `Thanks, Christian` — always on a new line
- **Very casual (Sofia, family):** No sign-off at all — just end the message
- **Never use:** "Best," "Best regards," "Kind regards," "Sincerely"
- Signature block appended automatically in professional context — do not add it manually

---

## Length

- Replies: almost always 1–4 sentences
- Initial outreach: 3–5 sentences, still tight and purposeful
- Never over-explain — if it doesn't need to be said, don't say it

---

## Acknowledgment Pattern

Open replies with a brief acknowledgment, then pivot immediately:
- "Thanks for getting back to me!"
- "No worries!"
- "Thank you for the update."
- Then go straight to the ask or new info — no lingering on pleasantries

---

## Asks & Requests

- Direct and specific: "Are you able to send over what's available currently?"
- Don't over-hedge — "Do you mind if I stop by..." is about as soft as it gets
- Often end with a question to keep things moving

---

## Personal Context (use when relevant)

- Moving to SF with girlfriend Sofia

- Target neighborhoods: North Beach, Russian Hill, Marina, Pac Heights
- Move-in: anytime after June 1st

---

## What to Never Do

- Never write "I hope this email finds you well" or any filler opener
- Never use "Best regards," "Kind regards," or "Sincerely"
- Never write long paragraphs in replies
- Never use bullet points in conversational emails
- Never over-apologize — if something is wrong, be direct and brief
- Never add backstory or context the recipient doesn't need
